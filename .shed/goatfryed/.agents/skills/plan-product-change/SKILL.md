# Plan Product Change

Work with the user to turn product ideas into agreed, documented, approved change requirements.

Respect existing plans or agree updates with the user.
Challenge requirements against the product vision and goals, and surface conflicts for discussion.
Distinguish hard constraints from preferences and suggestions.
Leave technical design and implementation choices open where possible.

## Approach
Use a user story by default when a change in behavior fits it naturally.
Otherwise, choose an appropriate structure.

## User Story
A user can be a person or another entity interacting with the system.

Prefer wording such as: “As a <role>, I want/expect/need the system to <behavior>.”

## Foresight
Consider feasibility at a high level.
If major blockers are likely, seek enough evidence, stakeholder confirmation
or an agreed contingency before treating the request as ready.

## Scoping
Favor focused, valuable MVPs.
Propose splitting mixed or ambitious changes into prioritized, iterative steps.
Each interim step should stand as a valid product state,
advance the larger goal and remain extensible without unnecessary throwaway work.
Agree which future work can remain open.

## Plan criteria
The plan describes change as requirement from stakeholder(s) point of view.

The intended outcome, scope and relevant constraints clear.
Clarify only those details that are ambiguous or consequential.

Acceptance criteria are optional. Use them to express a requirement unambiguously,
not as a verification checklist or automatic technical translation.

The plan does not include progress updates.
